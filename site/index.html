Fine-tune vs. retrieval · one model, three systems

Yu-Gi-Oh SLM Arena

One Yu-Gi-Oh question, three systems answering live — the untouched Gemma 2 2B base, our QLoRA fine-tune, and the fine-tune with a retriever feeding it real passages. Each answer is scored 0–10 by a reference-grounded blind judge that is handed the gold answer, so the scores are checkable. It all tests one claim: fine-tuning teaches the answer's shape; retrieval supplies the facts.

A reference-grounded, blind, pointwise judge

Every held-out question ships with a gold answer and its source evidence. The judge (Gemini flash-lite) is handed that reference and grades each answer against it — so the scores are checkable, not vibes. It scores one answer at a time and is never told which system produced it (blind), which removes position and identity bias.

The rubric · 10 points

  • Correctness · 5 Does the answer match the gold fact? The heaviest weight — this is what separates the systems.
  • Completeness · 2 Does it cover the whole answer, not just part of it?
  • Groundedness · 2 Is every claim supported by the evidence? Invented facts force this to 0.
  • Clarity · 1 Is it readable and direct?

Two guardrails make the score honest: groundedness collapses to 0 the moment the model fabricates, and a refusal ("I don't know") beats a confident wrong answer — so bluffing is punished, not rewarded. Systems are then compared with a paired test (same 60 questions each), using a bootstrap 95% CI as the primary signal, cross-checked with a paired t-test and Wilcoxon.

What the model was trained on

The fine-tune's supervised set is 2,683 grounded QA pairs, distilled by a teacher LLM from a curated Yu-Gi-Oh corpus and filtered through a blind-judge gauntlet. That corpus is two free sources: Yugipedia editorial prose (CC BY-SA 4.0, attributed) and YGOPRODeck card facts. Card effect text is © Konami, used only as labelled fair-use context — never a verbatim-recall target.
